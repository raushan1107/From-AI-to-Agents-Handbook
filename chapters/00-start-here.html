Let's make sense of it

This book is not organised as a glossary. It is organised as a journey. We begin with the problem humans were trying to solve, follow the breakthroughs that made modern models possible, and then assemble those models into applications.

Connect the concept

The recurring question is: what problem does this idea solve? When a new term appears, place it in the architecture before memorising its definition. The running example is Northstar Services, a fictional support organisation with manuals, customer messages, invoices and ticketing tools.

Read Part I to understand the foundations. Part II explains model-powered applications. Part III adds retrieval, tools and agents. Part IV explores language, speech, vision and production concerns. You can jump to a chapter later, but the concepts make more sense in sequence.

A model is one component—not the whole application. A useful AI solution also needs data, instructions, orchestration, evaluation, security, monitoring and a clear user experience.

Trainer's takeaway

Do not memorise this as an isolated definition. Explain the problem it solves, how it works at a high level, what it connects to, and what can go wrong.
